# "Tongue(s) of Fire": A Cross-Linguistic Dossier

*The metaphor `lšn` "tongue (of fire)" from Ugarit to Acts 2:3 — primary texts in original languages with translations, and the witnesses where the metaphor is **expected but absent**.*

---

## 1. The thesis in one paragraph

West-Semitic `lšn` ("tongue") names not only the speech-organ but anything *tongue-shaped* (an ingot, a strip, a spit of land, a flame-tip) and the *licking / devouring* action of such things. From this polysemy grows a fixed idiom — **"tongue of fire"** (`lᵉšôn ʾēš` / `leššānā d-nūrā`) — that runs from the cosmic, sky-licking tongues of Ugaritic myth, through the Hebrew prophets and the Aramaic of Qumran and the Targums, into the apocalyptic throne-visions of *1 Enoch*, and finally onto the heads of the disciples at **Acts 2:3** (`γλῶσσαι ὡσεὶ πυρός`). Greek (γλῶσσα) happens to carry the same triple sense — shape / lick / language — which is exactly what Luke exploits when the fiery *tongues* yield speech in foreign *tongues*. Crucially, the metaphor is **not universal**: where a translator found it un-idiomatic, it is flattened (LXX `ἄνθραξ` "coal"; Targum Isaiah `bᵉʾeštā` "fire") — and tracking *those* losses is as informative as tracking the survivals.

## 3. Part I — Where the metaphor IS attested

### 3.1 Ugaritic — the substrate: the licking, devouring, cosmic tongue
*(Not yet "of fire," but the source-image: plural tongues that lick the sky / a tongue that spans to the stars and devours.)*

**KTU 1.83 I:5–6** — the cosmic dragon **[CUC]**
```
lšnm tlḥk / šmm
```
> "(its) two **tongues** (`lšnm`) **lick** (`tlḥk`) the heavens."
Plural *tongues* + the verb *lḥk* "to lick" + reaching the sky — the whole knot, a millennium before Acts.

**KTU 1.5 II:2–4** — Môtu (Death) as all-devouring maw **[CUC]**
```
špt l arṣ špt l šmm / yšt lšn l kbkbm / yʿrb bʿl b kbdh
```
> "(one) lip to the earth, (one) lip to the heavens, he sets (his) **tongue to the stars**; Baʿlu enters his belly, goes down his mouth…"
The mouth/lip/**tongue** triad (`p ∥ špt ∥ lšn`, so del Olmo Lete *DUL* s.v. *lšn*).

→ Hebrew cognate of this maw-image: **Ps 73:9** `שַׁתּוּ בַשָּׁמַיִם פִּיהֶם וּלְשׁוֹנָם תִּהֲלַךְ בָּאָרֶץ` "they set their mouth in the heavens, and their **tongue** ranges the earth" (verb *šatû* ≈ Ug. *yšt*) **[SEF]**.

### 3.2 Hebrew Bible — the tongue becomes *fire*

**Isaiah 5:24** **[BHSA/SEF]**
```
לָכֵן כֶּאֱכֹל קַשׁ לְשׁוֹן אֵשׁ וַחֲשַׁשׁ לֶהָבָה יִרְפֶּה
```
> "Therefore, as a **tongue of fire** (`lᵉšôn ʾēš`) devours (`ʾkl`) stubble and as hay sinks down in flame…"
*The prime locus: the licking/devouring tongue is explicitly fire.*

**Isaiah 30:27** **[BHSA/2°]**
```
… שְׂפָתָיו מָלְאוּ זַעַם וּלְשׁוֹנוֹ כְּאֵשׁ אֹכָלֶת
```
> "…his lips are full of indignation, and **his tongue is like a devouring fire** (`ûlᵉšônô kᵉʾēš ʾōkālet`)."

*Related "fire-voice" (different lexeme, same theophany): **Ps 29:7** `קוֹל יְהוָה חֹצֵב לַהֲבוֹת אֵשׁ` "the voice of YHWH hews flames of fire" **[SEF]**.*

### 3.3 Qumran — "tongues of fire" as a fixed plural (Hebrew **and** Aramaic)

**4Q204 (4QEnochᶜ ar) 1 vi 22, 28 = *1 Enoch* 14:9–10** **[DSS]**
```
ולשני נור סחרין סחור סחור להון … ועלת אנה בלשני נורא אלן     (l. 22)
… וכלה מתבנא בלשני נור                                        (l. 28)
```
> "…and **tongues of fire** (`lšny nwr`) encircling them round about… and I entered into those **tongues of fire**." / "…and the whole of it (the heavenly house) built with **tongues of fire**."
*The Aramaic Vorlage of the Greek below.*

**4Q206 (4QEnochᵉ ar) 1 xxi 3** **[DSS]** — `בלשני נור` (parallel copy).

**4Q530 (4QEnGiantsᵇ ar) 2 ii 9, 12 — Book of Giants** **[DSS]**
```
… חזא הוית עד די לשנין די נור מן …          (l. 9)
… מתאבדה בלשנין די נור ובמיא די מבולא       (l. 12)
```
> "I watched until **tongues of fire** (`lšnyn dy nwr`) [came] from…" / "…being destroyed by **tongues of fire** and by the waters of the flood."

**11Q10 (11QtgJob) 36:5 = MT Job 41:11** **[DSS]**
```
יפקון בלשני אשה ירטון ׃ מן נחירוה יפק תנן
```
> "they go forth, with **tongues of fire** (`lšny ʾšh`) they dart; from his nostrils goes forth smoke."
*The Aramaic Job-targumist renders MT `kîdôdê ʾēš` "sparks of fire" as "tongues of fire" — see the contrast in §4.*

**4Q376 (apocrMosesᵇ / "Three Tongues of Fire") 1 ii 1 — Hebrew, oracular** **[DSS]**
```
… ויצא עמו בלשנות אש ׃ האבן השמאלית אשר על צדו …
```
> "…and it came forth with him **in tongues of fire** (`lišnôt ʾēš`). The left-hand stone which is on its side…"
*Tongues of fire flashing from the high-priestly stones (Urim/Thummim) to deliver an oracle — the closest Second-Temple structural parallel to Acts 2: fire-tongues + divine speech.*

**4Q381 (Non-Canonical Psalms) 24 2** **[DSS]**
```
ולשני כגמר … ואין מכבה
```
> "and **my tongue like a live coal** (`gemer`)… and none can quench it." (Cf. the coal on the prophet's lips, Isa 6:6–7.)

*Note: within the Scrolls all three senses of `lšn` coexist — geographic "tongue" of land/sea (1QapGen 16–17), **fire** (above), and **language/nation** `עממיא אמיא ולשניא` (4Q112 = Dan 7:14; 1QIsaᵇ = Isa 66:18).*

### 3.5 Rabbinic — the one voice split into seventy tongues, given in fire

**b. Shabbat 88b** **[SEF]**
```
כל דיבור ודיבור שיצא מפי הגבורה נחלק לשבעים לשונות …
תני דבי ר׳ ישמעאל: "וכפטיש יפצץ סלע" — מה פטיש זה נחלק לכמה ניצוצות …
```
> "Each utterance that went forth from the mouth of the Almighty **split into seventy tongues** (`lᵉšônôt`)… The school of R. Ishmael taught: '…and like a hammer that shatters a rock' (Jer 23:29) — as the hammer breaks into many **sparks**, so each utterance split into seventy tongues."
*Fire (hammer-sparks) + division into tongues, fused.*

**Shemot Rabbah 5:9** **[SEF]**
> "'All the people **saw the voices**' (Exod 20:15) — voice is not written, but *voices*. R. Yoḥanan said: the voice went forth and **divided into seventy voices, into seventy tongues**, so that all the nations might hear… spoken **from the midst of the fire** (Deut 5:22)… to each according to his strength."
*The Sinai matrix Acts 2 reworks (one Spirit → divided fiery tongues → many languages, "to each one of them").*

*Cf. the haggadot of **fire encircling Torah-scholars "as at Sinai"** (Deut 4:11) — pChag 2:77b; Midrash HL 1:10 (Ben Azzai, R. Akiva, R. Abbahu); Sukka 28a (Jonathan b. Uzziel burns the birds overhead) — gathered in Strack–Billerbeck, *Kommentar* II.602–604 **[2°]**, the experiential bridge to Pentecost.*

### 3.6 Aramaic Targum — a freshly coined "tongue of fire"

**Targum Sheni Esther 6:13** **[SEF]**
```
… אֶלָּא נְפַק לִישָׁנָא דְנוּרָא מִגּוֹ אַתּוּנָא וְאוֹכִיל לְמַאן דַּאֲכַלוּ קַרְצֵיהוֹן
```
> "…but a **tongue of fire** (`lišānā d-nurā`) went out from within the furnace and **devoured** (`ʾkl`) those who had slandered them."
*The Daniel-3 furnace: the tongue of fire harms the accusers, not the righteous — the same "harmless-to-the-elect / lethal-to-the-rest" logic as Shemot Rabbah's two-faced voice, and as the harmless flames resting on the disciples.*

### 3.8 New Testament — Acts 2:3

**Acts 2:1–4 (Greek)** **[SEF/2°]**
```
… καὶ ὤφθησαν αὐτοῖς διαμεριζόμεναι γλῶσσαι ὡσεὶ πυρός …
καὶ ἤρξαντο λαλεῖν ἑτέραις γλώσσαις …
```
> "…and there appeared to them **divided/distributed tongues as of fire** (`διαμεριζόμεναι γλῶσσαι ὡσεὶ πυρός`)… and they began to speak in other **languages** (`γλώσσαις`)…"

### 3.9 Comparative Semitic (Akkadian)

**The lexeme — verified in Old Babylonian (literal sense only)** **[AKK-OB]**
The noun *lišānum* "tongue / language" is attested in the OB corpus, but only literally — never as "tongue of fire":
```
P372937 obverse 6:   1(diš) lú li-ša-nam lú Idamaraz
```
> "one man, a *lišānam* (a 'tongue' = interpreter/dragoman), a man of Idamaraz."
(Also broken at P313328 rev. 15 `li-ša-an-ni`.) The 77 OB / 16 OA forms beginning *li-ša-* are otherwise overwhelmingly **precative verbs** (*lišābil* "let him send," *lišaddin* "let him give," *lišakīn* "let him place"), not the noun.

→ **Diagnostic negative:** the *fire*-metaphor `lišān girri/nūri` is **absent from both the Old Babylonian and Old Assyrian corpora** — expected, since these are early-2nd-millennium letter/legal/commercial archives. The metaphor below is a **1st-millennium (Neo-Assyrian)** phenomenon, in genres not represented here.

**The metaphor — Neo-Assyrian, supplied apparatus** **[2°]** *(not in the loaded corpora)*
- `lišān girri` "tongue of fire" — Esarhaddon, *Borger Esarh.* 104:6 (RINAP Q003262): *lišān gīri munnaḫzi ana Uppume ušaḫḫirma* "I turned a tongue of spreading fire against Uppume."
- `lišān nūri(šu)` "tongue of (his) light/fire" — *mīs pî* ritual, K.1279 ii 12–14: *lišān nūrišu kīma birqi ittanabriq* "the tongue of his radiance flashes like lightning."
- `lišānu` = the separate **tongue/tip of a torch-flame** in omen texts (CT 39, 35:39–40, etc.); also "tongue" = ingot/blade (AHw 556; CAD L 209ff.).

**Classical Greek — Aeschylus, *Agamemnon*** **[AESCH]** — flame is a **beard**, never a tongue.
The beacon-fire speech (this edition's card 281 ≈ standard line 306):
```
… πέμπουσι δʼ ἀνδαίοντες ἀφθόνῳ μένει
φλογὸς μέγαν πώγωνα, καὶ Σαρωνικοῦ
πορθμοῦ κάτοπτον πρῶνʼ ὑπερβάλλειν πρόσω
φλέγουσαν· …
```
> "…kindling with ungrudging force they send onward **a great beard of flame** (`φλογὸς μέγαν πώγωνα`), so as to overpass, still blazing, even the headland that overlooks the Saronic strait…"

Across the whole play `γλῶσσα` occurs **9×** (1, 636, 681, 810, 1017, 1202, 1372, 1617, 1649) — every one meaning **speech / language** (e.g. the proverbial *ox on the tongue*), **none** of fire. `φλόξ` (4×) is paired with `πώγων` "beard," not `γλῶσσα`. So even in high tragic diction the Greek somatic metaphor for flame is the *beard*; "tongue of fire" is **not** native Greek idiom — it enters Greek as a Semitic loan-image (*1 Enoch*, Acts).

**Classical Latin** **[2°]** — likewise no fixed "tongue of flame," **but** the *image* of a harmless flame resting on a head as an omen of election is Roman: Virgil, *Aen.* 2.682–684 — *tactuque innoxia mollis / lambere flamma comas* ("harmless flame **licks** [the boy's] hair"); cf. *Aen.* 7.71–77; Livy 1.39. So Acts 2:3 joins a **Semitic lexicon** (`lᵉšôn ʾēš`) to an **omen-iconography** its Greco-Roman readers also knew.

**Standard Rabbinic Targum of Job** **[—]** — *not verified here* (Sefaria has no "Targum Jonathan on Job"; the Hagiographa Targum is a separate, later witness). The Qumran **11QtgJob** (§3.3) is the Job-targum witness that attests "tongues of fire."

**Reading the matrix.** The idiom is robustly **Semitic** and conserved by the **Syriac** tradition; **Greek** systematically declines the somatic metaphor — the LXX uses coal / hearth / torch / oracle, and even tragic poetry (Aeschylus) calls a flame a **"beard"** (`πώγων`), reserving `γλῶσσα` for speech alone. The **Jewish Aramaic** tradition is split — it *flattens* the metaphor at Isa 5:24 yet *coins* it freshly at Tg. Sheni Esther and (at Qumran) even *imports* it into Job where the Hebrew had only "sparks." On the **Akkadian** side the lexeme *lišānum* is old and common but stays literal in the 2nd-millennium archives; the *lišān girri* "tongue of fire" metaphor surfaces only in 1st-millennium Neo-Assyrian sources. Acts 2:3 inherits the live Semitic idiom and the Greek pun on `γλῶσσα` at once — a Semitic image wearing a Greek word.
